# Boundless Backer: Home Page Suggestions

These are recommendations for a future round. Nothing here has been built. The current, approved home page structure is unchanged.

## The main observation

Boundless Backer's core promise is "Fund the art you want to see in the world." Today the home page reads mostly like a bookshop: it shows finished books, but not the crowdfunding, the community behind it, or the real-world impact. The suggestions below close that gap so a first-time visitor understands within seconds that they are backing art, not just buying a book.

## Suggested new sections

### 1. Live campaigns (highest priority)
A "Back a live project" section directly after the hero.

- 2 to 3 campaign cards, each with cover image, title, short line, funding progress bar, percentage funded, number of backers, days left, and a "Back this project" button.
- Candidates from the pitch deck: *Exploring Sanātana Dharma*, *Urpi Means Paloma*, *Chaos Diamond*.
- Why: crowdfunding is the business model, and visible progress creates urgency and social proof.
- WordPress: WooCommerce products plus a crowdfunding plugin; the progress bar is a simple, reusable component.
- Needed from the client: which campaigns are live, goals, amounts raised, end dates.

### 2. Trust strip (partners and press)
A thin row of muted partner logos directly under the hero.

- Partners named in the pitch deck: MoMA, the Dalí Museum, Newslink.
- Optional line: "Backed by a collaboration of 140+ muralists, including Kenny Scharf and Ron English."
- Why: instant credibility at very low design cost.
- Needed from the client: permission to show each logo, and the logo files.

### 3. Impact stories
Expand the current single "25,000+" figure into three short proof points, each with a photo and one sentence. For example:

- 25,000+ copies sold.
- Thousands of educational books donated to rural schools in Peru.
- A library rebuilt in Cambodia.

Why: one number tells people *how much*; stories tell them *why it matters*. This follows the brand's documentary photography direction.
Needed from the client: confirmed figures and real photos from these projects.

### 4. Founder and mission
A short "Why we exist" block with a photo of Diego Orlandini and two or three lines:

- Social entrepreneur and author.
- Architect of the first local coloring book movement in Wynwood.
- Curator of the world's largest street art book collaboration (140+ muralists).

Why: people back people. A real face builds trust, especially for crowdfunding.
Needed from the client: approved photo and bio wording.

### 5. "Publish with us" call to action for artists (optional)
A slim dark band: "Have a book in you? Crowdfund it with Boundless Backer," with one button.

Why: artists are a second audience. Today "Crowdfund your book" is hidden inside one of four tiles.
Needed from the client: confirmation that artist submissions are open, and where the button should lead.

## Suggested cuts and merges

- **Intro band and quote repeat each other.** Both are brand statements. Keep one: either the gold tagline band under the hero, or the large quote near the end. Fewer, stronger sections feel more premium.
- **"Become a guest" and "Become a host" are unclear** to a first-time visitor. Either add one line explaining each programme, or move them to a dedicated page.

## Proposed order (if all suggestions are approved)

1. Hero
2. Trust strip *(new)*
3. Live campaigns *(new)*
4. Books worth backing (shop)
5. How it works
6. Impact stories *(expanded from the current impact section)*
7. Reviews
8. Founder and mission *(new)*
9. Ways to participate
10. Newsletter
11. Footer

Removed in this order: the separate quote section (or the intro band, whichever the client prefers to keep).

## Decisions needed from the client

1. Which of sections 1 to 5 to add.
2. Which brand statement to keep: tagline band or quote.
3. What to do with "Become a guest" and "Become a host".
4. Content for any approved sections (campaign data, logos, impact figures and photos, founder photo and bio).

Every approved section can be built first with clearly marked placeholder content, then filled in once the material arrives.
